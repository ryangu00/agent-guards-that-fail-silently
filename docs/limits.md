# Limits and open questions

The source evidence is historical. Offline success of the public fixtures establishes their specified behaviour, not production acceptance or the effectiveness of a guard in another environment.

- **Selection bias.** The 25 cases were discovered failures. The duration sample's median 6 days and seven cases at 47 days or more do not estimate undiscovered failures. The one-time audit found cases 5, 6, 7 and 22.
- **Setting.** One operator's macOS/launchd setup, host version 2.1.158 and second-agent CLI 0.153.4 constrain portability. The historical Mac's model was not supplied. The cookbook's supplied hardware label does not substitute for that missing fact.
- **Canary coverage.** Four red records in 85 days were real faults, but the unseen-fault denominator is unknown. The retirement rule above 3 days median repair time and the 14-day shadow rule have almost no data behind them: the history contains two outages. Three-injection coverage was not demonstrated guard by guard.
- **Ownership.** Index drift failed on 10 of 19 recorded runs between 2026-09-16 and 2026-10-03 for real reasons, without action. No demonstrated mechanism makes an owner consume an alarm. The separate stop-the-producing-line policy is not reproduced here.
- **Closeout effect.** There were 387 fires, 8 cleared debts and 7 clean transition records. Scheduled sessions accounted for 38% of fires. No labelled false-positive rate, quality measure or improvement in discipline was established.
- **Receipt residuals.** A same-name executable earlier on PATH, reuse of a real slug, shell writes hidden inside programs and poor-quality records remain. The five forgery forms came from one reviewer family; this was not an open-ended red team.
- **Reference-gate evidence.** The surviving round-five report lists 85 subprocess commands and return codes; the raw JSON was temporary and is gone. The toy reproduces classes, not the exact original findings. Of 11 round-five findings, three trust-anchor findings were declared out of scope, five were listed as small follow-up fixes, and three—the self-attested closeout, duplicate lane record and write-scope check—were not mentioned in the decision record. The full fast lane was not merged.
- **Review counts.** The 14 findings / 12 adopted, 12 / 10, and 36 / 24 counts are the orchestrator's adoption accounting, not independently audited measurements. Reviewers were also language models; their involvement does not convert an assertion into execution evidence.
- **Regex scope.** Historical results were recorded on Python 3.14.6 and BSD grep 2.6.0. Python has warned on nested sets since 3.7. GNU grep differs on bracket/backslash handling. The public runner reports its actual results, which need not match the historical table; full build and invocation details for reconciling a difference were not supplied.
- **Open design questions.** Can probes be generated from the registration matrix? Who watches the canary beyond the 72-hour stale line? Is 14 days the right shadow length? How should ledger-free guards be probed, given that two of 14 wired hooks lacked ledgers? Separate-user or signature-protected anchors were designed but not built.
- **Public-plugin consistency.** The canary lesson retains 47 days, same-evening repair and main-path degradation from the gate's first day. The public plugin's live compliance page was not checked online. The frozen case-24 wording remains "1 of 8 within the window, not attributable to the advice"; no causal benefit or current effectiveness claim is made.

The scripts cannot establish a real scheduler run, runtime policy visibility, live gateway credentials, database source scope or an independent verifier identity. Those prerequisites must be supplied by a deployment. The toy deliberately demonstrates why locally rewriting both a manifest and its hash still passes the hardened variant.
